# Verdict: both coffee candidates — REJECTED

These were the only two products from discovery round two that cleared freight and economics. Both are now rejected, for different reasons, and the second reason is the more important one.

## Manual burr hand grinder — REJECT on intellectual property

This reproduces exactly the pattern that killed three earlier products.

Comandante's founder, Bernd Braune, is the named inventor on a family of **US design patents covering non-electric grinder bodies and components**: USD1,044,404 "Component of a non-electric coffee grinder" and USD700,007 "Coffee grinder", with USD1,005,061 and USD1,040,610 sitting in the same title space. Two independent trade sources report that Comandante **initiated proceedings against Kingrinder in April 2024** over burr geometry and sent cease-and-desist notices to other brands, with earlier 2022 actions reportedly involving 1Zpresso and Timemore.

Design patents are the fastest way a listing dies, because Amazon's Design Patent Neutral Evaluation turns on **how the product looks**, and every generic hand grinder looks like a Comandante. There is no version of this product that is both sellable and visually distinct enough to be safe.

Timemore's own "patented S2C burr" marketing claim could not be corroborated with any US patent number. It may be a Chinese utility model, which would not bind a US seller, or it may be unsupported. Either way it does not help us.

Separately, Fellow Industries holds **US 12,369,751 B2** on conical burr grinders, granted 2025-07-29, with a full twenty-year runway.

## Hand-press espresso maker — REJECT, and the deciding factor is safety

### The intellectual property picture was survivable

Wacaco's "patented pumping system" claim resolved to a real number: **US 9,918,582 B2**, "Portable manually-operated coffee maker", filed 2014, granted 2018, reported active, running to roughly 2034. Wacaco publicly states it will "vigorously investigate" copiers and holds registered NANOPRESSO and MINIPRESSO marks.

But **no Wacaco litigation or takedown was found anywhere**. That is meaningfully different from K9 Sport Sack, which is suing right now. Armed but not observed firing. Claim 1 could not be read, because every patent full-text database is blocked from this environment, so scope is unknown and scope is the whole question. That was resolvable with a few hours of attorney time.

### What is not resolvable with an opinion letter

There is a **2026 CPSC recall of coffeemakers imported by Kidisle**: at least **107 reports of unexpected hot liquid or steam release and at least 27 injuries, including first and second degree burns requiring medical treatment**. Sold on Amazon, Walmart and eBay between June 2024 and April 2026 at around $49.

That is the same product class, the same price band, the same marketplace channel, and a small importer. It is the closest analogue to this business model found anywhere in the entire project.

The recurring failure mode across historical espresso recalls, Krups, Braun, Mr. Coffee, Hamilton Beach, IKEA, is a **portafilter or filter holder dislodging under pressure**. A hand press loads that exact joint by design. And no applicable safety standard for a non-electric hand-pressurised brewing vessel could be identified; electric coffee-maker standards do not obviously reach it.

### Why that decides it

We would be selling a hand-pressurised vessel of near-boiling water, manufactured by a factory we cannot inspect, under the owner's own name, through the same channel and at the same price as a product recalled this year for burning 27 people.

Intellectual property risk is a business risk, and an opinion letter prices it. A scald injury to a customer is not a business risk that can be priced away, and the owner has no controlled supply chain and, at this stage, no product liability cover. Paying an attorney to clear claim 1 would be money spent on a product that should be declined regardless of what the answer came back as.

**Rejected on safety exposure, independent of the IP question.**

## Where this leaves the shortlist

Both round-two survivors are gone. What replaces them is not nothing, because the constraint that killed the other six has just been removed.

The **cast-iron lever citrus press** is now the strongest remaining candidate:

| Factor | Position |
|---|---|
| Intellectual property | **Cleanest in the project.** Patents found are expired: USD407954 and US5193447B1. The lever-press form is early twentieth century prior art, US2238571 from 1941 |
| Demo | Excellent. Half an orange in, one lever pull, juice out. No explanation needed |
| Demand | Weakly sustained. Two independent creators 227 days apart, plus broad multi-retailer distribution including foodservice |
| Air freight | Killed it. $24 per unit on 4 kg |
| **Sea freight under bulk** | **Revives it with enormous headroom: $3,436 per cubic metre affordable against typical rates in the low hundreds** |
| Safety | A hand lever and a metal cone. No pressure vessel, no heat, no electricity |
| Incumbent | No dominant brand anchoring below $60 found |

Note the contrast with the espresso press. Both are manual kitchen devices at a similar price. One applies force to hot pressurised water, the other squeezes a piece of fruit. That difference is the whole safety argument.

## Still running

Sea freight rates and the bulk cost model, discovery round three scoped to products that were uneconomic only on air freight, and the sourcing channel ladder. The citrus press should be re-screened for IP properly before it is recommended, since its clearance so far came from a general discovery pass rather than a dedicated screen.
