# Sourcing playbook: where the money actually is

A caveat that applies to nearly every number below. The sourcing, freight and inspection figures come from companies that sell sourcing, freight and inspection services. They are marketing their own margins. Labelled CLAIM throughout and treated as directional.

The agent also **excluded two statistics that looked authoritative but could not be traced to any primary source**, one attributing 67% of import fraud to China-origin suppliers to CBP and one giving an average fraud loss of $87,500. Neither appears in any CBP publication and both surfaced only on search-optimised sourcing blogs. That is the right call and worth noting, because those are exactly the numbers that end up in a business plan unchallenged.

## The channel ladder

Price delta against an Alibaba.com international listing.

| Channel | Delta | Access requirement | Principal risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Factory direct | −20% to −50% | An agent, a referral, or a trip. Higher MOQ | No platform recourse at all |
| **1688.com** | −15% to −40% list, **−20% to −30% landed** | China-side counterparty: agent, forwarder with a Chinese address, or proxy buyer | No Trade Assurance equivalent, counterfeit exposure, domestic-spec goods that may fail US safety rules |
| Yiwu market | Roughly 1688 level on small commodities, worse on manufactured goods | Yiwu agent at 3 to 10% | You are buying from a stall operator, not a production line |
| Made-in-China.com | No consistent advantage found | Standard account | Thinner pool. Best audit transparency of the directories |
| Global Sources | **+12% to +18%** | Standard account | You pay a premium for claimed vetting |
| Alibaba.com | baseline | Nothing | Export markup priced in. About 90% of profiles claim to be manufacturers |
| AliExpress | +30% to +100% | Nothing | Not a bulk channel |

The real dividing line is not the website. It is whether you have someone in China acting for you. The top three rows require that; the rest do not.

## The levers, ranked

| Rank | Lever | Swing | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Order quantity** | 15 to 40% | Steepest between the first two tiers, roughly 28%. Tooling amortises from $0.80 a unit at 500 to $0.08 at 5,000 |
| 2 | **Channel choice** | 15 to 30% landed | Compounds, because duty falls with the goods value |
| 3 | **HTS classification and spec design** | 10 to 25 points of FOB | The most overlooked lever of 2026 |
| 4 | Spec and packaging simplification | 5 to 20% | Cuts goods cost and freight at once |
| 5 | Negotiation | 5 to 15% | Mostly derivative of levers 1 and 2 |
| 6 | Freight mode | Large ratio, small share | Only a top lever if currently air-freighting |

The ranking inverts for cheap bulky goods, where freight dominates, and for dense high-value goods, where duty does.

## Two things that compound, and are easy to miss

**Channel savings multiply through duty.** US duty is assessed on the goods transaction value, excluding international freight and insurance. So a lower ex-works price also shrinks the duty bill. In the worked example below, **30% of the total saving was duty never paid**. At a 40% duty line the same discount would be worth considerably more.

**Classification can beat sourcing.** The duty stack routinely runs 20 to 45% of the free-on-board price now that de minimis is gone. A correct classification, or a spec change that moves the product to a different tariff line, can be worth more than the entire 1688 discount. The instruction that follows is: **classify before you finalise the spec, not after.**

## Worked example, 500 units of a $15 item

| Line | Alibaba.com | 1688 with an agent |
|---|---|---|
| Goods, free on board | $7,500 | $5,250 |
| Agent commission at 8% | — | $420 |
| China domestic freight and consolidation | — | $120 |
| Currency conversion, about 2% | — | $105 |
| Pre-shipment inspection | — | $309 |
| Sea freight, 1.5 CBM | $375 | $375 |
| Customs brokerage | $150 | $150 |
| **Duty at 25% of FOB** | **$1,875** | **$1,313** |
| **Total landed** | **$9,900** | **$8,042** |
| **Per unit** | **$19.80** | **$16.08** |

A 30% discount at the factory gate becomes about 19% landed, because the agent fee, currency spread, domestic freight and inspection absorb roughly a third of it. On a like-for-like basis, inspecting both routes, the saving is closer to 22%.

At this order size the agent barely pays for itself on price alone. The reason to run the first order this way is to learn the real factory price and lock the specification. The money shows up on orders two and three.

## The recommended sequence for a first bulk order

1. **Classify before sourcing.** Get a licensed customs broker to give the tariff code and full duty stack on the candidate specification, before talking to any factory. A few hundred dollars, and the highest return per dollar available.
2. **Write the spec and sample from three channels.** Alibaba, an audited Made-in-China supplier, and a 1688 supplier via an agent. Retain a signed, sealed golden sample.
3. **Run order one on Alibaba with Trade Assurance, through platform checkout.** You will overpay by 15 to 30%. Buy it anyway: you are purchasing fraud protection while the counterparty is unknown, a documented spec in a purchase order, and the identity of a factory to return to. Keep it small enough that losing it would not hurt.
4. **Inspect before final payment**, against an agreed sampling standard and the golden sample. Roughly $309 a man-day, about 4% of a $7,500 order. Expensive as a percentage, cheap as insurance against the most common scam in the category.
5. **Move the reorder down the ladder and scale quantity together.** Engage an agent on a **flat fee rather than commission**, which removes the incentive to steer you toward expensive suppliers.

## Standing rules

- The receiving bank account must be a **corporate account whose registered name exactly matches the company on the contract and the business licence.** A request to pay a personal account, or a Hong Kong account for a mainland company, is the most reliable single fraud indicator there is.
- **Never accept a mid-order change of bank details by email.** Verify by voice call to a number you already had. This one catches sophisticated buyers.
- **Keep inspecting repeat orders.** Quality fade specifically targets the buyer who inspected once and stopped looking.

## What Trade Assurance is actually worth

It covers late shipment and goods that differ from what the purchase order documented. It does not cover goods you simply dislike, it only applies to orders paid through Alibaba's own checkout, and quality claims need inspection evidence to succeed.

Sources contradict each other on whether product conformity is genuinely covered, and Alibaba's own rule pages are unreachable from here. **Read the actual rule text before relying on the quality coverage.**

It is worth having on a first order mainly because it protects against the supplier vanishing with the money, which is the risk that actually matters when you do not yet know who you are dealing with.

## Telling a factory from a trading company

Trading company markup runs 15 to 25% on simple goods and 30 to 50% on complex ones. About 90% of Alibaba profiles claim to be manufacturers, so the claim carries almost no information.

The strongest tell before any conversation is **catalogue breadth**. A factory that makes Bluetooth speakers does not also make kitchen appliances, LED lights and power tools. After that, ask for the business licence: a manufacturer's registered scope includes production, a trading company's says trade and sales.

Then four questions. A factory answers all four within a day; a trading company deflects at least two.

1. A live video walkthrough at a time **you** pick, not a recording.
2. Which processes are in-house and which are outsourced.
3. Confirmation that the name on the audit certificate matches the quote and the bank account.
4. One hard technical question with no commercial content: a tolerance, a material grade, a cycle time.

A good trading company is not the problem. Paying a trading-company markup while believing you are factory-direct is, because you then cannot control quality drift or negotiate the reorder.

## Open items
