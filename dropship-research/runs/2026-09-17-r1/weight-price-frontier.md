# The weight-price frontier, and what actually resolved

## Two risks cleared, one created

### Cleared: the flat per-parcel fee

This was the highest-priority open risk in the project, and the answer is clean. CBP guidance states that non-postal shipments file a normal entry in the Automated Commercial Environment and pay ordinary duties. The flat specific duty and the 54% alternative applied **only to items entering through the international postal network**, and the specific-duty option **expired 2026-02-28** in any case.

So a container is not a parcel, the fee never touched containerised cargo, and it no longer applies to anything.

### Cleared: the irreconcilable duty rates

The three figures that would not reconcile were three different things:

| Figure | What it actually was | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 54% or $100 flat | Postal parcel rate under the IEEPA-era order | Dead twice: postal-only, and IEEPA was struck down |
| ~30% | IEEPA fentanyl 20% plus reciprocal 10% | Struck down 2026-02-20 |
| ~37.5% | The Section 301 aggregate for goods on Lists 1 to 3 | Live, but not the rate for consumer goods |

For a **List 4A consumer hardgood**, which explicitly covers kitchenware and household goods, the stack is roughly 3.4% most-favoured-nation plus 7.5% List 4A plus 12.5% forced-labour baseline, giving about **23.4%**.

### Created: Section 232 steel at 50%

Cast iron may attract Section 232 steel duty on its metal content. That is the one thing sea freight might not rescue the citrus press from, and it is unresolved.

## Sea freight makes the China leg almost free

At roughly $137 per cubic metre, which is one unverified figure inside a broader $140 to $200 all-in range:

| Product | Air freight | Sea freight | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cast-iron citrus press, 4 kg | $24.00 | $0.55 to $0.80 | 97% cheaper |
| Rooftop cargo bag, 6 kg | ~$36 | $2.74 to $4.00 | 90% cheaper |
| Pet stroller, 15 kg volumetric | $90.00 | $10.98 to $16.00 | 85% cheaper |

Sea is never less than about eight times cheaper than air per unit at any density. The crossover is around 50 to 100 kg of total shipment, so any order of 300 units clears it by an order of magnitude.

## But the weight penalty simply moved to the US last mile

Priced at every stage, at $89 retail with a $10.50 ex-factory cost:

| Unit weight | Sea | US postage | 3PL and entry | Total shipping | Contribution | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 kg | $0.05 | $7.00 | $5.60 | $12.65 | $45.45 at 57% | PASS |
| 1.0 kg | $0.20 | $9.00 | $5.60 | $14.80 | $43.19 at 54% | PASS |
| 3.0 kg | $0.60 | $14.00 | $5.60 | $20.20 | $37.49 at 47% | PASS |
| **4.0 kg** | $0.80 | $16.00 | $5.60 | $22.40 | $35.17 at 44% | **FAIL** |
| 6.5 kg | $1.30 | $22.00 | $5.60 | $28.90 | $28.31 at 35% | FAIL |

**US outbound postage is 60 to 75% of total shipping cost. Sea freight is 2 to 5%.** The China leg was never the real problem once you buy in bulk. And postage is getting worse: an 8% fuel surcharge runs to January 2027 on top of a 7.8% general increase, about 16% cumulative year on year.

## The frontier

| Retail price | Heaviest product that still clears the gates |
|---|---|
| $79 | 1.0 kg |
| $89 | 3.0 kg |
| $99 | 5.0 kg |
| $119 | 6.5 kg |

This is the single most useful rule the project has produced. It replaces guesswork about whether a product is "too bulky" with a number, and it should govern every future candidate.

## What it does to the citrus press

At 4 kg it needs **$99 or more**, and if Section 232 applies to cast iron it needs **$119**.

| Retail | 23.4% duty | 40.9% duty | With steel at ~63% |
|---|---|---|---|
| $89 | FAIL, ROAS 2.28 | FAIL | FAIL |
| $99 | PASS, ROAS 2.05 | PASS, ROAS 2.15 | FAIL |
| $119 | PASS, ROAS 1.79 | PASS, ROAS 1.85 | PASS |

Observed retail for the comparable Eurolux press was **$72.19**.

So the press needs to sell at $99 to $119 against a $72 incumbent. That is the same structure that killed the paw cleaner: asking a cold-traffic buyer to pay well above the established market price. The difference in degree matters, since a 37% premium is not the 100% premium the paw cleaner needed, and the category does carry premium models above $100. But it is the same shape of problem and it should not be waved through.

## What bulk actually costs

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| 500 units of a $15 ex-factory item, all-in | $11,800 to $14,000 |
| Landed per unit at 500 | about $23.60 |
| Minimum sensible order | 300 units, below which fixed entry costs distort everything |
| Deposit to first shippable unit | about 70 days West Coast, 90 East Coast |
| Deposit to last dollar recovered | **8 to 10 months** |
| 3PL monthly minimum | about $517, payable whether or not anything sells |

About 90% of the goods cost leaves 20 to 45 days before the stock is sellable, and duty is due about a week before it reaches the warehouse.

**Spend authorisation remains zero. None of this has been committed.**

## Open items

1. Classify the 8-digit HTS code for any candidate before modelling it. The swing between 23.4% and 40.9% is material, and Section 232 on steel would be worse.
2. Confirm whether cast iron triggers Section 232. This alone decides the citrus press.
3. The $137 per cubic metre figure rests on a single unverified snippet. Use the $140 to $200 all-in range until three written quotes exist.
4. It is currently peak season for both air and ocean, so any quote taken now sits near the top of the annual cycle.
