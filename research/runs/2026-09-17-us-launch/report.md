# Product research report — run 2026-09-17-us-launch

US general consumer, Shopify target, paid ads from day one, spend authorization
zero. Five agents: discovery, competitor, supplier, economics and risk, and this
lead. All figures dated 2026-09-17.

---

## The headline, stated before the detail

**Recommendation: the collapsible multi-compartment car trunk organizer,
P-2026-09-17-002.** It is the only candidate of thirteen that passes both the
price-band gate and the economics gate, and it is clean on intellectual
property, safety and advertising policy.

**But no product can be called a finalist yet, including that one.** Gate G5,
tracked US delivery within twelve days, is `unknown` for all four survivors,
because every supplier catalogue is unreachable from this environment. Under
the rubric an unknown gate is a hold, not a pass. Two specific inputs close it,
both listed at the end.

I am recommending a direction, not declaring a winner. The difference matters
when the next step costs money.

---

## What this research could and could not establish

This environment's network proxy is an allowlist. Only code infrastructure is
reachable; every commerce, government and research domain is refused at the
connection layer. Server-side search still reaches those sites, so the work
below rests on domain-scoped search snippets with URLs and dates, not on pages
anyone opened.

Obtained at fact grade: retail prices with named models and URLs, customer
complaint themes in near-verbatim language, CPSC recall records with incident
counts, patent numbers, and published platform advertising policies.

**Not obtainable at any grade:** supplier unit costs, real Google Trends curves,
sales volume or Best Sellers Rank, review counts and rating distributions,
competitor advertising activity, and verified shipping transit times.

So demand is unverified for every candidate in this report. That is normally a
central input. Rather than dress up snippets as a demand signal, the ranking
rests on what is actually checkable: price-band fit, unit economics headroom,
demonstration value for paid creative, safety and IP exposure, and shipping
physics.

### 1. Car trunk organizer — recommended

Observed retail $29.99 to $39.99 across three named competitors, all inside the
band. Target price $34.99.

The economics have the widest headroom of the four. Maximum landed cost is
$13.65 at the base case, still $8.93 if you require $5 profit per order, and
**positive at $5.64 even in the pessimistic scenario**. It is the only candidate
that survives the pessimistic case at all. Break-even cost per acquisition is
$12.60.

Intellectual property is clean. The collapsible cargo organizer patents found,
US6817504B2, US6938807, US7104582, US6979037 and US7784632B2, are 2002 to 2008
filings, so their twenty-year terms have expired. This is a screen, not legal
advice.

No CPSC recall exists for car or cargo organizers. The storage recalls that
surfaced all involve rigid hinged lids closing on children or suspended garage
racks, neither of which describes this product. Not restricted by Meta, TikTok
or Shopify.

Customer complaints are specific and consistent, which is what makes a
differentiation angle credible rather than invented:

- The base and dividers are flimsy; the organizer collapses into itself when
  lightly loaded.
- Units "disintegrate" within weeks of normal use.
- Plastic tension-buckle straps pull apart under minor force.

Those map onto three angles: a reinforced stiffener base and heavier divider
walls, metal rather than injection-moulded buckle hardware, and a four-corner
anchor with a non-slip base. The first two require sourcing a better unit; the
third is demonstrable on camera immediately.

The real operational risk is fit. The organizer has to suit the buyer's specific
boot, and "too big for my car" is not a defect return but still costs the full
contribution plus return freight.

### 2. Aluminium laptop stand — viable, tighter

Retail $23.39 to $59.99, median $36.23, one competitor $1.61 below the floor.
IP, safety and policy all clean.

Economics are the problem. Maximum landed cost is $11.71 at the base case,
$6.99 with $5 profit required, and **only $4.67 in the pessimistic case**. For a
0.7 to 1.2 kg anodised metal item shipped from overseas with tracked delivery,
that is tight enough that G6 is marked unknown rather than pass. Shipping weight
is the single biggest threat to the ceiling and is the one input nobody has
measured.

Complaints are strong and specific, though: wobble under active typing, laptops
slipping off short support arms, instability on soft surfaces.

### 3. Over-sink dish rack — weakest of the survivors

It has the best five-second silent demo of the four, which matters for paid
creative. Everything else is against it.

**The strongest single risk finding in this run is here.** Two design patents
match the product description word for word: **D1023495, "Adjustable over the
sink dish drying rack"**, and **D630813, "Multiple tier over the sink dish
rack"**. That is not a loose category hit, it is the product name. Design
patents protect ornamental appearance, so whether a particular sourced rack
infringes depends on a visual comparison that could not be made here, because
the drawings and grant records sit behind blocked domains. D1023495's number
falls in the 2024 issue range and post-2015 design patents run fifteen years
from grant, so it is probably live. That inference is low confidence and is not
a fact. Gate G2 is therefore `unknown`, and it must be cleared by a real
design-patent search before a single unit is ordered. This is a screen, not
legal advice.

Everything else also argues against it. No exact price could be confirmed
despite ten dedicated searches across four retailers, so G8 is unknown and the
$35 target is assumption-grade. It has the worst shipping profile of the four:
rigid, does not fold flat, and carries a dimensional-weight penalty the
arithmetic does not show. Its likely HTS classification sits in the steel
household-articles family, where 2025 Section 232 steel-derivative tariffs could
stack on the base rate and consume the ceiling alone. Rust and coating chips
appear in the complaint record and typically surface *after* the return window
closes and *inside* the chargeback window, so they arrive as disputes rather
than returns. Return freight on a rigid multi-kilo item usually exceeds the
whole contribution on the order, which makes a refund worse than a lost sale.

### 4. Vacuum compression bags — rejected

Fails two gates outright. Confirmed competitor pricing is $10.99 for a five
piece set and $12.96 for a six pack, both far below the $25 floor; only a
fifteen piece bundle at $25.99 barely clears it. Maximum landed cost at $25 is
$9.37, which fails G6.

It had the best shipping profile of the four and was the supplier agent's first
sample pick on that basis, which is exactly why gates run before scores. A
product cannot be rescued by being cheap to ship if it cannot carry the price.

Two findings to carry forward if it is ever reopened. **SPACE BAG is a
registered trademark in exactly this category**, so that phrase and Ziploc must
never appear in a title, advert or store name. And the category owns a real
hazard: valve patent text itself notes that a lost diaphragm creates a
suffocation risk to a pet or infant, so a suffocation warning and a
keep-out-of-reach line would be mandatory, not optional.

**How to reopen it:** re-test at about $34.99 backed by competitor price
evidence, which would move the base ceiling to $13.65. At $25.00 there is no
room, because that is the floor of your price band.

## Rejected at discovery, with reasons

Nine of thirteen candidates were rejected before deep research. Each has a
written reason in `products.jsonl`. The substantive ones:

- **Resistance bands with door anchor.** Two independent grounds: $16.99 retail
  is below the floor, and CPSC shows a recurring recall pattern across five
  incidents since 2011 for handles and anchors detaching and striking users,
  including about 95,000 units in 2020.
- **Adult weighted blanket.** A 15 lb item against an $18 ceiling; freight
  plausibly consumes it alone. CPSC also recalled about 204,000 children's
  weighted blankets in 2023 with two fatalities, an adjacent category that makes
  imagery and copy a permanent compliance burden.
- **Clothes drying rack.** Bulky rigid item; the ceiling is implausible against
  its shipping volume and rigid frames arrive bent.
- **Cooling memory foam pillow.** Sleep products carry high try-before-trust
  return rates, and returns destroy the computed margin.
- **Slicker brush, packing cubes, magnetic wristband, dog seatbelt harness.**
  All below the price floor on observed retail; the harness additionally carries
  animal-safety liability unsuitable for a first product.
- **Yoga mat.** Lowest demonstration value of the set at 2 of 5, which is the
  wrong bet when paid ads run from day one.

## Gate G7, the evidence floor, fails on all four

This is my gate rather than any agent's, and it is the reason none of these can
be called a finalist.

G7 fails when a key economic input is unknown. Landed cost is unknown for all
four products, so contribution margin was never computed for any of them. The
G6 verdicts above use the substitute test, the maximum-landed-cost ceiling,
which is a genuine decision tool but is not the same test the rubric specifies.

Stated plainly: **the economics of every product in this shortlist rest on a
number nobody has.** The ceilings tell you what a supplier quote must beat. They
do not tell you that any supplier meets it.

## Unresolved questions

1. **Real landed cost for the trunk organizer.** The entire economic case rests
   on sourcing under $13.65 landed. Nobody has a quote.
2. **HTS classification for the trunk organizer.** Genuinely unresolved and it
   matters: a fabric-bodied unit may classify around 17.6% base duty, a rigid
   plastic one far lower. Competitors use both constructions. Duty comes out of
   the same $13.65.
3. **Tracked delivery time.** Unverified for every product. This is gate G5.
4. **Demand.** No trend, rank or volume data was obtainable by any means here.
5. **Complaint frequency.** Themes are solid; how often each occurs is unknown,
   and no percentage was invented.

## What would close this

Two options, either sufficient:

**Option A, five minutes of your time.** Open one supplier listing for a
collapsible car trunk organizer and tell me three numbers: unit price, shipping
cost to a US address, and whether the quote is DDP or DDU. If the first two add
to under $13.65 including duty, the economic case holds and this moves to a
sample.

**Option B, environment access.** Run this work where the network is not
restricted to code infrastructure, and the agents complete the supplier,
delivery and demand research themselves.

Until one of those happens, calling this product validated would be a claim the
evidence does not support.
