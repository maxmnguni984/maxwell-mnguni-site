# Supplier inquiry

**Status: draft. Nothing has been sent to anyone.**

Fill the bracketed placeholders once the funnel names a product, then send to three suppliers separately. Do not send the same message as a group, and do not tell any supplier who else you are asking.

---

## How to use this

**Send it cold, without a price anchor.** Do not say what you hope to pay. The first number should come from them.

**Send it to three at once, separately.** One quote is not a market. Three lets you see whether the spread is 10% or 300%, and a wild outlier usually means one of them misunderstood the specification.

**The questions are ordered deliberately.** Questions 1 to 4 are ordinary commercial ones that any supplier answers easily. Questions 5 to 9 are the ones that separate a factory from a trading company reselling someone else's goods, and a vague answer to any of them is itself the finding. Question 10 is the one most first-time buyers forget and most regret.

**What a good reply looks like:** specific numbers rather than ranges, a carton specification given without being chased, willingness to name which processes are in-house, and a named corporate bank account matching the company on the quotation. A supplier who answers 11 of 12 questions crisply and goes vague on exactly one is telling you where the problem is.

## The message

> Subject: Product inquiry — [PRODUCT], quotation request
>
> Hello,
>
> I am sourcing [PRODUCT] for the United States market and am evaluating three suppliers. I am not looking for the lowest price; I am looking for the supplier who gives the most accurate answers, because I intend to reorder.
>
> Please answer each numbered question directly. Where you cannot give a firm figure, say so rather than estimating, and tell me what you would need in order to firm it up.
>
> **1. Samples.** Can you supply a sample of the current production version? What is the sample cost, what is the shipping cost to the United States, and how many days from payment to dispatch? Is the sample from existing stock or made to order? If made to order, is it from the same tooling and the same materials as a production run?
>
> **2. Minimum order quantity.** What is your MOQ for this item? Is that per order or per colour, per size and per variant? If I order two variants, does each need to meet the MOQ separately? What MOQ would you accept for a first order from a new customer, and at what unit-price premium?
>
> **3. Price at quantity.** Please quote your ex-works unit price at exactly 300, 500, 1,000 and 3,000 units. Give each as a single number, not a range. State the currency and how long the quotation is valid.
>
> **4. What is included.** Does that price include the retail packaging, the inner packing, and the manual or insert? If not, quote each separately. Is the price ex-works, FOB, or DDP, and from which port or city?
>
> **5. Customisation.** Can you apply my logo and my own retail packaging? What is the minimum quantity for custom packaging, what is the one-off plate, film or tooling cost, and is that cost refundable or creditable against future orders? Can you ship with no supplier branding, no third-party marks and no supplier invoices inside the carton?
>
> **6. Lead time.** How many working days from payment to the goods being ready for carrier collection, at 500 units and at 3,000 units? What is your current order backlog? How does Chinese New Year 2027 affect this timeline, and what is your latest order date before that shutdown?
>
> **7. Packed dimensions — please be exact.** For a 500-unit order, give me: the individual retail box dimensions in centimetres and the unit weight in grams; the master carton dimensions in centimetres; the number of units per master carton; the gross weight per carton in kilograms; and the total cubic metres for the order.
>
> *(This is the single most important commercial answer in this message. Shipping cost in my market is driven by packed size more than by weight, so a supplier who packs the same product into a smaller carton is materially cheaper to me even at a higher unit price.)*
>
> **8. Factory or trading company.** Are you the manufacturer of this item, or do you source it? If you manufacture, which processes are performed in your own facility and which are outsourced? Would you be able to show me a live video walkthrough of the production line at a time I propose?
>
> **9. Verification.** Please send a copy of your business licence, and confirm that the company name on the licence matches the name that will appear on the quotation, the invoice and the bank account. Please also confirm the registered business scope shown on the licence.
>
> **10. Defects and returns.** What is your stated defect allowance as a percentage? If units arrive with [LIKELY DEFECT 1] or [LIKELY DEFECT 2], do you replace, credit or refund? Do I need to ship defective units back to you at my cost, or do you accept photographic evidence? If a customer in the United States returns a unit to me, what is your process, and do you accept any liability at that point?
>
> **11. Test documentation.** For the United States market I need the following in writing before I can place an order: [TEST DOC 1], [TEST DOC 2], [TEST DOC 3]. Can you supply these test reports for the exact material and specification you would ship, with the report showing the product name, the batch or lot number and the test date? Are these your own reports or a third-party laboratory's, and which laboratory?
>
> **12. Payment and inspection.** What payment terms do you offer a first-time customer? Would you accept a third-party pre-shipment inspection arranged and paid for by me, with the balance payable after the inspection passes?
>
> I would appreciate a reply to each numbered point. If it is easier, reply inline beneath each question.
>
> Thank you,
> [NAME]

## Placeholders to fill before sending

| Placeholder | Source |
|---|---|
| `[PRODUCT]` | The funnel's recommendation, described precisely enough that a factory knows which item you mean. Include material and size |
| `[LIKELY DEFECT 1]` and `[LIKELY DEFECT 2]` | The two most common complaints found in competitor reviews. Naming the specific failure mode signals you know the product and makes a vague reply conspicuous |
| `[TEST DOC 1-3]` | The compliance documents the supply research identifies as actually required. Do not invent these. If the research says UNKNOWN, ask a customs broker before sending, or ask the supplier what they normally provide for the US market and verify it separately |

## Reading the replies

**Answers that should end the conversation.** A request to pay a personal account, or an account in a different name or country from the company on the quotation. A refusal to send the business licence. A price that is dramatically below the other two, which usually means a different specification. An offer to skip the inspection.

**Answers that mean "trading company", which is not automatically disqualifying but changes the price you should accept.** A product catalogue spanning unrelated manufacturing processes. Deflection on which processes are in-house. Reluctance to do a live video walkthrough at a time you choose. A business licence whose registered scope says trade or sales rather than production.

**The thing to check that nobody thinks to check.** Compare the packed-carton answer across all three suppliers. The same product can differ by 40% in cubic metres depending on how it is packed, and that difference flows straight into your shipping cost on every unit forever.

**What you still will not know.** A quotation is not a contract, a sample is not a production run, and a test report for one batch is not a guarantee for yours. The inquiry narrows the field. Only a sample and an inspection tell you what you are actually buying.
