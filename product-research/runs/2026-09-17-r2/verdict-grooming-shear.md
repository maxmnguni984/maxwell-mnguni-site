# Verdict: REJECT

sufficient, and the failure is arithmetic rather than judgement.

| Chris Christensen Classic 7.5in curved | price UNKNOWN | "Convex blade... Made From 440C Japanese Steel" |

All FACT-via-summary. The $69 target asks a groomer to pay a **130% premium
over a listing whose copy is word-for-word identical**, with no brand.

The competitor nobody flagged is the worst one: Chris Christensen is a name

Geib markets its Entrée line as "a low cost, high quality shear with a convex
edge" — which is the proposed product's entire pitch, from a legacy trade brand.

## 2. Acquisition: break-even sits below every published benchmark

Contribution before advertising **is** the break-even customer acquisition cost.
At $69 retail it is $22.29, a break-even ROAS of 3.10.

| Benchmark | Figure |
|---|---:|
| Median DTC Meta purchase ROAS | 2.96 |
| Median cost per purchase | $49.04 |
| Median CPA, all industries | $38.19 to $38.99 |
| Ecommerce average CPA | $29.99 |
| By channel: Meta / Google / TikTok | $42 / $38 / $51 |

All FACT-via-summary. **The cheapest benchmark in the set exceeds the ceiling by
35%.** At the median DTC outcome the founder loses $1.02 an order before taking
a salary. "Break-even CAC $22.29" reads like a target; it is in fact the
fiftieth-percentile-and-worse boundary.

**And Meta deleted the targeting this depends on.** Job title, industry and
company size targeting are gone, and ad sets still relying on them stopped
delivering on 15 January 2026. A product whose buyer is defined by their
occupation is no longer reachable on paid social.

## 3. Differentiation: no reason to buy, and the service wedge is taken

The obvious answer to "why an unknown brand" is to bundle the recurring cost.
That is occupied. Shear Integrity offers "FREE Lifetime Sharpening (with a UPS
Pre-Paid Return Label)"; Magix offers a free first sharpening; Precision Shears
and Scissor Mall offer lifetime warranties. Subscription models are already
running at $48 to $49 a month. A new entrant arriving with free sharpening
arrives third.

The one genuinely unserved gap is a **loaner shear during the 7-to-14-day
sharpening turnaround** — real, documented, and unoccupied. It is also
unaffordable: premium convex sharpening is $35 plus roughly $15 round-trip
shipping against a $22.29 contribution. One service event is negative margin
before any loaner inventory exists.

And the buyer is documented as price-resistant by their own trade press.
*Groomer to Groomer* reports the sharpening trade's core problem as "groomers
being unwilling or unable to pay the $30 or more for shear sharpening that
beauty salons pay."

## 4. As a dropshipping product specifically, it is worse

A dropshipped generic shear is the identical object the customer can buy
themselves on the same marketplace, at the same delivery time, for $22 to $30.
There is no version of that which is a business.

## On price and quality, since it was raised

Nothing here infers quality from price, and nothing infers it from a stamped
material grade. "440C Japanese stainless" appears on the $22.27 listing and on
the $69.99 listing alike, which is precisely why the label carries no
information. What the label is evidence of is what a seller is willing to claim.

Separating the three honestly:

| | What settles it |
|---|---|
| **Evidence** | independent review counts, order counts, seller history, third-party test reports, return-rate data |
| **Seller claim** | material grade, hardness figure, "forged", "hand-honed", country of origin |
| **Only a sample settles** | actual HRC hardness, actual steel composition, edge geometry and retention, pivot tolerance, finish |

A cheaper shear is not proven worse. It is **unproven either way**, and the only
thing that resolves it is a physical sample and a hardness or composition test.
