## The rule every line below follows

Each claim is one of three things, and it is labelled:

- **SHOWABLE** — provable on camera with the actual product in hand.
- **CONTINGENT** — true only if the sourced unit genuinely has that spec.
  Cannot be published until a sample confirms it.
- **NEUTRAL** — a factual description that needs no proof.

Nothing is published as fact until a sample confirms it. No testimonial, review
or customer quote appears anywhere in this document, because we have no
customers. Inventing one would be fraud, not marketing.

---

## Positioning

The category's problem is not that trunk organizers do not exist. It is that
the cheap ones collapse. Three complaint themes recur across competitor
listings, in buyers' own words:

1. The base and dividers are flimsy; the organizer collapses into itself when
   lightly loaded.
2. Units "disintegrate" within weeks of normal use.
3. Plastic tension-buckle straps pull apart under minor force.

Every one of those is a structural failure, not a feature gap. So the position
is structural, and it is narrow on purpose:

> **The trunk organizer that holds its shape empty.**

That single sentence is testable in five silent seconds: an empty organizer
standing upright while a full one collapses beside it. It is the whole creative
strategy, and it is why this product suits paid traffic.

### What we deliberately do not claim

- No crash-safety or cargo-restraint claim. It is a storage box, not a
  restraint, and implying otherwise invites both liability and an ad rejection.
- No lifetime guarantee. We have no track record to back one.
- No "military grade", "indestructible" or similar. Unfalsifiable claims read as
  noise and attract policy attention.
- No comparison naming a competitor brand.

## Brand direction

The brand name is not set, and should not be until you choose the product. Three
directions, with the trade-off stated rather than hidden:

| Direction | Example shape | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | something naming the boot or cargo space | Easy to rank for, hard to extend beyond one product |
| Structural | something naming rigidity or holding shape | Matches the positioning exactly; narrower |
| Neutral house brand | a short invented word | Extends to a catalogue later; carries no meaning at launch |

**Recommendation: neutral house brand.** A first product is a test, and a name
welded to trunk organizers becomes a liability the moment the test says move on.

Before registering anything: check the US trademark register, check domain
availability, and check that the name is not in use by a seller in the same
category. Neither register is reachable from this environment, so that check is
yours or a later session's.

Tone: plain, specific, slightly blunt. The product's appeal is that it does not
fall apart. The writing should sound like that.

## Product page copy

### Title
Collapsible Car Trunk Organizer with Reinforced Base

### Subtitle
Holds its shape when it is empty, not just when it is full.

### Opening paragraph
Most trunk organizers work fine loaded and fold into a heap the moment you take
the shopping out. This one has a stiffened base and reinforced divider walls, so
it stays standing between trips. NEUTRAL, with CONTINGENT on the stiffener.

### Benefits

1. **Stays upright empty.** CONTINGENT on a stiffener board in the base.
   Demonstrable once confirmed: stand it empty, push it, film it not folding.
2. **Metal buckle hardware on the tie-down straps.** CONTINGENT. The complaint
   record is specific that plastic buckles pull apart, so this is worth paying
   for at sourcing and worth showing on camera.
3. **Four-corner anchoring plus a non-slip base.** SHOWABLE. Film it holding
   position through a hard turn and a sharp stop.
4. **Folds flat when you want the boot space back.** SHOWABLE.
5. **Wipe-clean lining.** CONTINGENT on material.

### Specifications

Left deliberately empty. Dimensions, weight, material and fold size all come
from the supplier and none has been verified. Publishing a guessed dimension on
a product whose main return risk is "too big for my boot" would be actively
harmful.

**Before publishing, confirm and fill:** external dimensions, folded
dimensions, internal compartment count and sizes, weight, base material,
stiffener type, strap and buckle material, and load rating.

### Fit guidance, to sit above the buy button

Fit is the main return driver, so this belongs on the page rather than buried in
a policy:

> Measure the flat floor of your boot before ordering. This organizer needs
> [WIDTH] by [DEPTH] of flat space. If your boot floor is shorter than that, it
> will not sit properly, and that is the most common reason people send one
> back.

Dimensions pending the supplier spec.

## FAQs

Drawn from the actual complaint record, not invented. Answers marked pending
cannot be published until a sample or a supplier quote settles them.

**Will it collapse when empty?**
That is the specific problem this one is built to avoid. The base is stiffened
rather than plain fabric. CONTINGENT, confirm against the sample.

**Will it slide around in the boot?**
It anchors at four corners and the base is non-slip. SHOWABLE.

**What are the straps and buckles made of?**
PENDING supplier confirmation. We will not guess, because plastic buckles
failing is a top complaint in this category and the answer is the reason to buy.

**Will it fit my car?**
PENDING dimensions. Measure your boot floor first; fit is the most common reason
for returns.

**Can I put heavy things in it?**
PENDING a load rating from the supplier. We will publish the number they give
and say it came from them.

**How long does delivery take?**
PENDING. Currently unverified, which is exactly why it is not stated anywhere on
the site yet.

**What if it arrives damaged?**
Send a photo within 14 days. Replacement or refund, and we pay return postage.
NEUTRAL, this is our own policy.

## Advertising drafts

**None of these may run.** Paid advertising needs budget approval, and spend
authorization is currently zero. These are scripts held for review.

All three are built on the same silent five-second demo, because that is the
only creative idea the evidence actually supports.

### Concept 1: the side-by-side
Two organizers in a boot. Groceries removed from both. One folds into a heap.
Ours stays standing. Text on screen: "Empty. Still standing."
Under five seconds, no voiceover, works muted.
Requires: our own footage, our own product, and a genuinely comparable
competitor unit. We buy that unit; we do not use their footage.

### Concept 2: the buckle
Close on hands pulling a plastic buckle apart, then the same pull on a metal
one. Text: "This is why the cheap ones fail."
CONTINGENT on actually sourcing metal hardware. If the sourced unit has plastic
buckles, this concept is dead and must not be filmed.

### Concept 3: the hard stop
Boot camera. Hard braking. A loose organizer slides and spills; ours does not
move. Text: "Anchored at four corners."
Filmed safely and legally, on private ground, at low speed. It shows the
organizer staying put. It does not claim to secure a load or improve safety.

### Rules binding all creative
- Our own footage of our own product only.
- No competitor logo, packaging or footage on screen.
- No staged failure of a competitor product presented as typical. Showing a
  generic cheap organizer collapsing is fair; implying a named brand always does
  is not.
- No before-and-after body imagery, no health claims, no urgency countdowns that
  reset, no fake scarcity.
- Every on-screen claim traceable to this document and marked SHOWABLE.
